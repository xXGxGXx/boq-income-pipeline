Evidence auditors often look for:
- a risk register and safeguard map for each home, with dated reviews;
- incident records with actions taken;
- training in recognising and responding to abuse, neglect and exploitation;
- ways for participants to raise concerns safely, including people outside the provider.

Questions to test practice:
- What would a worker do tonight if they suspected a participant was being harmed by another resident?
- How do participants who don't use speech tell you something is wrong?

Evidence auditors often look for:
- a practice governance framework showing who oversees quality and safety in each home;
- supervision records;
- training records, and competency assessments for the practices the Rules require (check which ones);
- reports to the governing body;
- how lessons from incidents and complaints change practice.

Questions to test practice:
- Who checks that what the policy says actually happens on a Saturday night shift?
- How do you know each worker on the roster is competent for the supports they provide?

Evidence auditors often look for:
- service agreements kept separate from tenancy or accommodation agreements;
- a record of how each participant was supported to understand both, and their choices;
- how conflicts of interest are managed where you provide both housing and supports;
- what happens to a person's home if they change support provider.

Questions to test practice:
- If a participant wants a different SIL provider, what happens to their housing?
- How was this participant supported to understand their agreements?
