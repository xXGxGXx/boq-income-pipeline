## Step 1: Confirm your pathway

Since **1 July 2026**, Supported Independent Living must be delivered by a registered provider, under **registration group 0138 (Assistance with supported independent living)**. Find your situation:

| Your situation | What it means | Your next action |
|---|---|---|
| Already registered for group 0115 (daily tasks in shared living) | The Commission updates your certificate to include 0138 | Meet Module 5A now. Expect it to be assessed at your next audit |
| Not registered, but lodged a registration application before 1 October 2026 | You may continue delivering SIL while the Commission assesses your application | Prepare for your certification audit now (Steps 2 to 7) |
| Not registered, and did not apply by 1 October 2026 | You must not deliver SIL | Get advice. Plan participant transitions safely. Apply as a new provider if you intend to deliver SIL |
| New provider | You must be registered before you start delivering SIL | Apply for registration, then follow Steps 2 to 7 |

## Step 2: Know what will be assessed

- **The Core Module** of the NDIS Practice Standards (rights and responsibilities; governance and operational management; provision of supports; support provision environment).
- **Module 5A** (Schedule 7A of the Rules), the SIL supplementary module, with four standards:
  1. Supported decision-making
  2. Safeguarding
  3. Practice governance
  4. Agreements about tenancy, housing and support arrangements
- **The audit type is certification.** You engage an NDIS Commission-approved quality auditor. A certification audit generally has two stages: a document review, then a review of practice that includes interviews with workers and participants and samples of records. Confirm the scope, stages and timing with your auditor.

Copy the official outcome and quality indicators for each Module 5A standard into the self-assessment workbook. Use the exact wording, because that is what the auditor works from.

## Step 3: Self-assess honestly (week 1)
1. Open `module-5a-self-assessment-workbook`. For each standard, paste in the official quality indicators.
2. For each indicator, record:
   - the evidence you already have, and where it is;
   - a rating (Met / Partly met / Not met);
   - the gap, the action to close it, an owner and a due date.
3. Do the same for the Core Module at a higher level. Many providers already have most Core evidence.
4. Copy every evidence item into `evidence-tracker.xlsx`.

Tip: ask a support worker and, where appropriate, a participant or their supporter how things actually work day to day. Their answers are the best test of whether a policy is real.

## Step 4: Build or refresh your documents (weeks 2 to 4)

| Need | Start from |
|---|---|
| Supported decision-making policy and procedure | Prompt 03; template `supported-decision-making-record` |
| Safeguarding policy, risk identification and safeguard map | Prompt 04; template `safeguarding-risk-register` |
| Practice governance framework (oversight, supervision, competency) | Prompt 05; template `induction-and-competency-record` |
| Service agreements kept separate from tenancy arrangements | Prompt 06; templates `service-agreement-outline` and `tenancy-support-separation-checklist` |
| Incident management | Prompt 07; template `incident-register` |
| Complaints and feedback | Prompt 10; template `complaints-and-feedback-register` |
| Continuous improvement | Prompt 08; template `continuous-improvement-register` |

Every document needs an owner, a version number, an approval date and a review date. Keep a document register; the evidence tracker can serve as one.

## Step 5: Show it working in practice (weeks 3 to 10)
Auditors look for **evidence of daily practice**. Aim to have, for a sample of participants and workers:

- **Supported decision-making:** records of decisions made with participants, in their preferred way of communicating, including who supported them and how their will and preferences shaped the outcome.
- **Safeguarding:** a current risk register and safeguard map for each home, with recent reviews and actions taken.
- **Practice governance:**
  - induction records;
  - training and **competency assessments**: check the Rules for which practices workers must be trained in and assessed as competent in, and in particular whether supported decision-making is one of them;
  - supervision notes;
  - Worker Screening Check verification for every risk-assessed role.
- **Agreements:** signed service agreements kept separate from tenancy or accommodation arrangements, with evidence that participants understood them and could choose.
- **Incidents, complaints and improvement:** registers showing how each item was handled, what was learnt, and what changed as a result.

## Step 6: 30-60-90 day plan

| By day | Milestones |
|---|---|
| 30 | Pathway confirmed. Self-assessment done. Gap list owned and dated. Document register started. Auditor shortlisted |
| 60 | Core Module and Module 5A policies approved. Workers inducted and trained on them. Registers in daily use. First competency assessments done |
| 90 | Internal mock audit using the audit-day checklist. Gaps closed or under a dated plan. Auditor engaged and dates booked |

## Step 7: Audit-day preparation
Work through `audit-day-checklist` two weeks before your audit, and again two days before it. Make sure:
- documents can be found quickly;
- the right people are rostered to be available;
- participant and supporter interviews are voluntary, arranged with consent, and suit each person's communication needs.

## Step 8: Keep it running (after the audit)

| How often | What |
|---|---|
| Weekly | Review incidents and complaints. Check rosters against competency records |
| Monthly | Review risk registers. Spot-check supported decision-making records. Update the continuous improvement register |
| Quarterly | Report to the governing body on quality and safeguarding. Run training refreshers. Review and update policies due |
| Yearly | Full self-assessment against the Core Module and Module 5A. Review service agreements with each participant |

## Things this pack does not cover
- Regulated restrictive practices: behaviour support plans, state or territory authorisation, and reporting. Get specialist advice.
- Specialist Disability Accommodation (SDA) requirements, tenancy law in your state or territory, and the employment and work health and safety obligations that sit outside the Practice Standards.
